History

Who we are

IPAC — Instituto Pantanal Amazônia de Conservação (Pantanal Amazon Conservation Institute) — is a non-profit organization founded on June 25, 2004, at a meeting held at the Mato Grosso State University – UNEMAT (Tangará da Serra campus).

It was created by a multidisciplinary group of professionals — including lawyers, administrators, biologists, agronomists, teachers and technicians — to carry out environmental protection, preservation and restoration work, as well as cultural initiatives and the promotion of volunteering.

Since 2006, IPAC has served on public councils such as the Mato Grosso State Water Resources Council, the Sepotuba River Basin Committee and the Municipal Environment Council. It has also designed and implemented projects to protect and restore water resources, rural sanitation (bio-digester septic tanks), support for family farmers and the PCI strategy (Produce, Conserve and Include).

IPAC is officially recognized as a Public Utility Entity at the municipal and state levels. Learn more.

Mission, vision and values

What drives us

Environmental conservation is IPAC’s main goal: protecting natural ecosystems and biodiversity, mitigating climate change and promoting the sustainable use of natural resources.

Mission

To defend, preserve and restore the environment and cultural heritage, combining technical expertise, environmental awareness and community participation.

Vision

To be a reference in the conservation of water, soil and biodiversity in the Pantanal and the Amazon, so that every child grows up with the right to nature guaranteed.

Values

Sustainability, collaboration and education: balancing human needs with the protection of nature, working in partnership and valuing continuous learning.

Bylaws

Main goals

  • Protection, restoration, legal compliance, preservation and conservation of the environment, and promotion of sustainable development;
  • Promotion of culture and protection of historical and artistic heritage;
  • Promotion of education;
  • Promotion of volunteering;
  • Support for Indigenous peoples and traditional communities;
  • Promotion of economic and social development and the fight against poverty;
  • Non-profit experimentation with new socio-productive models and alternative systems of production, trade, employment and credit;
  • Projects, studies and research, development of alternative technologies, and production and dissemination of technical and scientific knowledge related to the activities above.

Recognition

Municipal and State Public Utility

The Public Utility title is an official recognition, granted by the legislature, that a non-profit organization provides services in the public interest. IPAC has received this recognition at two levels:

Municipal

Tangará da Serra, Mato Grosso

Granted by the Tangará da Serra City Council through Ordinary Law No. 5,327 of June 30, 2020.

State

State of Mato Grosso

Granted by the Legislative Assembly of the State of Mato Grosso through State Law No. 11,403 of June 1, 2021 (Official Gazette of June 1, 2021).

Services provided

Track record

  1. 2005

    Survey of the headwaters of the Sepotuba, Juba and Jauru rivers, with support from WWF-Brasil.

  2. 2006

    GEF Small Grants Programme project: training settlers of the Antônio Conselheiro settlement to grow orchid and heliconia seedlings with a focus on conservation and sustainability.

  3. 2006–2019

    Full member of the Mato Grosso State Water Resources Council (CEHIDRO), serving on the technical chambers for the State Water Resources Plan, Dams and Participatory Management.

  4. 2007

    Survey of the headwaters of the main rivers of the Pantanal headwaters arc (Cabaçal, Jauru, Juba, Formoso, Branco, Bugres, Sepotuba, Paraguai, Cuiabá, Manso and São Lourenço) and stakeholder analysis of the region, for WWF-Brasil.

  5. 2009–2010

    Participation in the review workshops of Brazil’s National Water Resources Plan in Brasília (2009), Cuiabá, Rio Branco and Fortaleza (2010).

  6. 2010

    Organization of the Sepotuba II Expedition (with SEMA/MT, WWF-Brasil and TV Centro América) and the Sepotuba III Expedition (with SEMA/MT, the Environmental Police and UNEMAT).

  7. 2010–2013

    Papers and motions at the National Meetings of River Basin Committees (ENCOB) in Fortaleza (2010), Cuiabá (2012) and Porto Alegre (2013), including the approved motion to create the Pact in Defense of the Pantanal Headwaters (Cuiabá, 2012).

  8. 2010–2014

    Helped create the Sepotuba River Basin Committee (CBH Sepotuba), which IPAC chaired from 2010 to 2014.

  9. 2013

    Coordinated the water quality assessment of the Jauru, Cabaçal, Sepotuba and Paraguai rivers for the Pact in Defense of the Pantanal Headwaters (WWF-Brasil).

  10. 2014

    Rural Quality of Life at the Pantanal Headwaters project (WWF-Brasil/HSBC), with 40 bio-digester septic tanks installed.

  11. 2014–2015

    Carried out the Payment for Environmental Services project in the Queima-Pé basin (WWF-Brasil) and took part in the Water Producer Project.

  12. 2019–2021

    Design and implementation of the Cultivando Vida Sustentável project – IDH (Netherlands) / CAT Sorriso.

  13. 2020

    Environmental quality assessment of the Lira River basin, in Sorriso, Mato Grosso, for the Sorriso city government.

  14. 2020–2021

    Water Production in the Lira River Basin project (installation of vertical drains), in Sorriso, Mato Grosso – CAT Sorriso / Mosaic Fertilizantes, with support from the city government and IFMT.

  15. 2021

    Restoration of the headwaters of the Queima Pé River, Tangará da Serra’s main water source.

  16. 2024

    Launch of the Cultivando Água Boa Tangará project at the springs of the Queima Pé and Ararão rivers.

At the invitation of Itaipu Binacional, IPAC also took part in the Annual Meeting of the Cultivando Água Boa Program and the Ibero-American Meeting on Sustainability in Foz do Iguaçu, Paraná.

How we work

Methodology

Work is carried out in teams, according to the needs of each situation. Field surveys are generally conducted and, based on the data collected, an Assessment Report is prepared with thematic maps, a description of each situation and photographic records.

The team includes a field agronomist, a field assistant, a technical draftsperson and a coordinating agronomist with a master’s degree in water resources.
