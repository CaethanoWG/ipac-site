Context

What the ECA Ambiental is

“ECA Ambiental” is the popular name of Brazil’s Law No. 15,512 of September 22, 2026, which sets out principles and guidelines for public policies that guarantee, with absolute priority, the right of children and adolescents to nature.

The name refers to the ECA, Brazil’s Statute of Children and Adolescents. The new law also amends three key laws: the Statute itself (Law No. 8,069/1990), the National Environmental Policy (Law No. 6,938/1981) and the National Climate Change Policy (Law No. 12,187/2009).

“Children and adolescents have the right to nature, which shall be fulfilled with absolute priority.” Law No. 15,512/2026, art. 2 (free translation)
In plain language

What the law guarantees

Under the law, the right of children and adolescents to nature includes:

1

Access to healthy, ecologically balanced natural areas, including squares, parks and rivers near home and school.

2

Family and community life, cultural activities and emotional bonds with nature.

3

Free play in contact with nature.

4

Nature-based education.

5

Protecting, conserving and restoring nature for present and future generations.

Absolute priority means, among other things, that children and adolescents come first for protection in environmental and climate risks, for access to natural areas and in environmental and climate policies. Young children, children with disabilities and those in vulnerable situations have even higher priority.

The law also guides urban planning: safe routes to school, squares and parks that encourage play in nature, “naturalized” play spaces, and warning systems and escape routes that children can understand during extreme weather events.

Schools and communities

Nature-based education

The law asks the federal, state and municipal governments to encourage nature-based education in schools. It is defined as combining environmental and climate education with strategies that foster access to and bonds with nature, biodiversity, the interdependence of all forms of life and climate resilience.

The law also encourages groups of children, adolescents and families working to protect, conserve and restore nature in their communities.

Awareness

Why awareness makes a difference

A law only becomes reality when people know about it. Children who grow up in contact with rivers, forests and springs learn to value them — and become the adults who will protect them. That is why informing families, educators and public officials is the first step to make this right real.

IPAC and the ECA Ambiental

Environmental awareness is at the heart of IPAC’s work. In the Cultivando Água Boa Tangará project, students from public and private schools take part in activities at the springs of the Queima Pé and Ararão rivers — tree planting, educational activities and following the recovery of the water that supplies the city.

Guidance

How you can take part

  • Families: take children to play outdoors, in nearby squares, parks and green areas.
  • Schools: include activities in natural settings in lesson planning and invite IPAC for environmental education.
  • Communities: care for local green areas and springs and follow municipal policies.
  • Public officials: consider children in urban planning and in environmental and climate policies.

In Brazil, violations of children’s rights can be reported to the Guardianship Council (Conselho Tutelar), the Public Prosecutor’s Office or by dialing 100 (Disque 100).

Questions

Frequently asked questions

Is the law already in force?

Law No. 15,512 was signed on September 22, 2026 and published in Brazil’s Official Gazette. For the effective date of each provision, check the official text on the Planalto legislation portal.

Who is responsible for guaranteeing this right?

The law assigns this duty to the federal government, the states, the Federal District and the municipalities, working together, as well as to families, communities and society.

Does the law also apply to companies and private organizations?

According to article 3, the law’s principles may be applied to the activities of the public sector and private entities. The law also mentions including children’s rights in corporate sustainability goals and reports.

What is an “urban blue area”?

Urban and peri-urban aquatic ecosystems — such as rivers, streams and lagoons — with ecological, landscape and recreational functions that improve people’s health and quality of life.
